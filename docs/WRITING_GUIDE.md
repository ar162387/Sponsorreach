# SponsorReach UK writing guide

## Position and audience

SponsorReach UK helps people identify relevant UK licensed sponsor companies and use available contact/application routes to approach them directly. The primary reader is an international graduate already in the UK on a Graduate visa, commonly called PSW. Other people seeking sponsorship can use the same professional intake.

Lead with the PSW deadline, the investment the reader has already made, and a concrete next step. The commercial value is a focused shortlist and access to available routes for outreach. Jobs, sponsorship and immigration decisions belong to employers and the relevant authorities.

## Voice

Use direct, persuasive British English. Be warm and practical, confident about what the product delivers, precise about what remains uncertain. Speak to the reader as “you” and describe what they can do next. Keep paragraphs short and make buttons name their action.

Approved headline: **Find UK sponsors before your PSW runs out.**

Approved supporting copy: **You’ve invested in your education and your future in the UK. Discover licensed sponsor companies relevant to your degree, skills and goals—with available contact details so you can reach out directly.**

Use visa timing as a real reason to start. Do not invent a remaining visa duration, a countdown, scarcity, response rates or employment outcomes. Do not imply that the user's date affects company ranking; optional visa context stays separate from professional matching.

## Vocabulary and taxonomy

| Meaning | Preferred label | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brand | SponsorReach UK | SponsorMatch in user-facing copy |
| Primary action | Find my sponsors | Get hired, secure your visa now |
| Results | Sponsor shortlist / sponsor companies | Job matches when they are company recommendations |
| Relevance explanation | Why this company may fit | This company needs you / perfect match |
| Licensing | Licensed Skilled Worker sponsor | Guaranteed sponsor / will sponsor you |
| Contacts | Available contact and application routes | Direct recruiter contacts unless established |
| Related results | Companies for related roles | Equivalent jobs |
| Vacancy | Verified open vacancy | Hiring now without verified evidence |
| Account | My sponsor shortlists | My employers |
| Optional context | Visa timing | Visa assessment |
| Unlock | Full sponsor shortlist | Sponsorship package |

Emails, phone numbers, websites, careers pages, company LinkedIn, LinkedIn jobs and job-board links are shown when supplied by accepted company information. Personal LinkedIn profiles are not promised. A company may have only some of these channels, and a contact does not guarantee deliverability or a reply.

## Claim boundaries

Keep three facts distinct: sponsor authorisation, professional relevance and an open vacancy. A licence authorises sponsorship on its listed route; it does not confirm willingness to sponsor a person. Relevance describes connections between published company work and the person's background. Vacancies appear separately when verified.

Say “up to three free matches when relevant results are available.” Never fill empty results with made-up companies or suggest that insufficient evidence means a candidate lacks potential. Demo companies, contacts and sources must remain visibly fictional and non-actionable.

Full access currently uses the existing demo unlock. The default £19 is configurable and illustrative. Say “Demo only. No card details. No charge.” Do not describe it as a completed purchase or subscription.

## Founder story

The founder reported contacting companies using scraped data, finding employers willing to offer jobs, and being unable to go while in Pakistan. Use this as a first-person founder anecdote, not an independently verified customer result. No invented name, photograph, employer quotation or sponsorship success.

Approved story: “I contacted companies using scraped data and found employers willing to offer jobs. I was in Pakistan at the time, so I couldn’t go. That experience showed me the value of reaching the right companies directly.”

The supplied video is context only. Its £40,000 degree cost, two-month visa timeline and narrated story are not general facts or testimonials to republish.

## Immigration wording

Use “Graduate visa (commonly called PSW)” on first explanatory use. A Graduate visa cannot be extended; a person may be able to switch to another route if its requirements are met. Sponsored employment requires the applicable job offer, Certificate of Sponsorship and visa conditions. Do not claim automatic extensions or guaranteed settlement. Settlement remains a conditional FAQ topic, not a sales benefit guaranteed by the platform.

Sources checked for this rebrand on 13 October 2026:
- https://www.gov.uk/graduate-visa
- https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa

Recheck official sources before changing immigration claims. Keep legal-operator name and privacy contact configuration intact; a brand is not a substitute for the configured legal controller.

## UI states

- Loading: name the genuine processing stage; avoid made-up accuracy or progress percentages.
- Failure: explain the step could not complete and offer a new search or saved shortlist.
- Empty results: explain that available company evidence is insufficient, then suggest a specific role, skills/responsibilities or related roles.
- Locked results: state the actual number of additional companies and the information unlock reveals.
- Unlocked demo: confirm the same saved shortlist was revealed and no payment was taken.
- Account: distinguish browser-only access from email sign-in without promising indefinite storage. Sessions expire after 30 days under the existing service policy.

## Copy review checklist

Does the reader know why to act, what they receive and what to do next? Are company relevance, sponsorship willingness and vacancies distinct? Are available channels, sources and demo access described truthfully? Is urgency based on visa timing rather than invented scarcity? Is the copy British English, readable on mobile and specific to sponsorship research?
